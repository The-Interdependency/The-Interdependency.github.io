# Need, contribution, harm and review

**Proposed pilot procedure · interpretation · not an adopted community rule.**

Feed people, make necessary work visible, stop specific harmful conduct, and make
every consequential decision answerable to someone other than its maker.
This procedure applies the existing Article Labs; it changes no canonical text.
It is a candidate for a voluntary community pilot. Publication supplies neither
participant consent nor authority over a person, their property or public space.

## Before using it

Participants must agree on the version, scope, resource-allocation rule and review
process. Name a rotating facilitator, a resource steward, two available reviewers
and an alternate. Record the names privately and make the roles and contact path
available to participants. A complainant, subject, supplier or person with a
personal stake cannot review their own dispute. If independent review cannot be
staffed, leave contested findings unresolved and pause new sanctions.

Explain the rule and its challenge path in a form each participant can use.
Permit spoken responses, a chosen supporter, translation and extra time. A
signature, silence or attendance does not establish understanding or consent.

## Keep the questions separate

| Question | What to record | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| What does the person need now? | Available food, water, shelter, rest and assistance; immediate shortages. | Meet the need or apply the agreed, need-based scarcity rule. Need is never a debt. |
| What can they currently do? | Their account of capacity, barriers, tools, time and support. No diagnosis is required. | Offer feasible choices, support, rest or reassignment. |
| What contribution occurred? | Task, time or occasion, result, and source of the account. Include care, maintenance and teaching. | Acknowledge the work and plan coverage. |
| What conduct caused harm? | Specific action, affected person or resource, time, consequence and evidence. Separate direct observation, report and inference. | Consider a proportionate boundary through review. |

For this pilot, **harm** means a specific impairment of bodily safety, a person's
boundaries or property, or the shared ability to provide necessities. Disagreement,
anger, poverty, disability, absence or an unwitnessed contribution does not by
itself establish harm. An allegation may justify immediate protective care before
it is resolved; care and a finding against someone are different decisions.

## Capacity and refusal

Ask privately what contribution is feasible. Offer a real choice and the necessary
tools, instruction, access and time. Record the person's response in their own
terms. They can decline a task. Silence, exhaustion, distress, missed schedules
and missing records remain unknown capacity or uncovered work, not deliberate refusal.

Record a **declined feasible task** only when the task, available support and
explicit response are known. This is task-specific; it is not a permanent label
for the person. Investigate recurring uncovered work as an allocation problem.
Refusal alone never withdraws the survival floor or licenses forced work.

## Contribution without continuous surveillance

Use a small task board: what is needed, who voluntarily took it, what support is
missing, and whether it is done. Keep personal needs and disputes off that board.
A participant can give a spoken or written account afterward. An optional witness
can confirm a particular event; no one needs continuous observation or a phone.

Mark accounts **self-reported**, **confirmed**, **disputed** or **unknown**. A missing
witness is not a zero contribution. A witness confirms what they observed; they
do not acquire authority to punish. Resolve a material conflict through review,
not a popularity vote. These records create no currency, transferable debt or
ranking of human worth.

## Stop harm, then decide

1. **Protect now.** Address immediate danger through the least restrictive feasible
   action: pause a task, separate an interaction or supervise access to a particular
   hazardous tool. Seek appropriate help when needed. Do not treat speech or prior
   conduct as permission for retaliatory violence or humiliation.
2. **Write the event.** Record the specific allegation, immediate measure, decision
   maker, start time, scope and expiry. Every temporary measure needs a review
   appointment within 24 hours and ends by that appointment unless reviewed. Missed
   review is a process failure, not automatic renewal. Renew only from a current
   specific risk; an unresolved past allegation alone is insufficient.
3. **Hear both accounts.** Give the affected person the allegation, evidence relevant
   to the decision and a supported chance to respond. Protect other people's private
   details. Permit a supporter and a correction to the record.
4. **Review independently.** Two uninvolved reviewers assess the action, consequence,
   available evidence, context, alternatives and conflicts. Agreement is required
   for a non-emergency restriction. Uncertainty remains explicit. The steward
   controlling supplies cannot settle the case alone.
5. **Give reasons and an end.** Prefer repair, task reassignment or a narrow access
   condition. Specify who does what, until when, and what ends the condition. Never
   withhold necessities as punishment, confiscate property or create a permanent
   ban through this procedure. Separate safe provision can protect others while
   meeting a person's needs. Scarcity must be recorded as scarcity.
6. **Allow challenge.** A participant can challenge a decision orally or in writing
   without retaliation. A fresh, uninvolved reviewer and an uninvolved retained
   reviewer hear it within 48 hours; the original decision maker recuses. Pause a
   contested restriction unless a current, specific safety risk requires a narrower
   temporary measure under steps 1–2. Missing independent review keeps the finding
   unresolved. Correct errors visibly to the affected parties and restore access
   or repair loss where possible.

## Minimum private record

Use one short record per consequential event:

```text
Case reference / date / procedure version:
Specific action and consequence; observation, report or inference:
Immediate need and provision; any actual scarcity:
Each person's account; missing evidence and support requested:
Conflict checks; facilitator and independent reviewers:
Temporary measure / start / expiry / review appointment:
Finding / reasons / uncertainty / repair and end condition:
Challenge route / response due / correction or closure:
Who may see this record / agreed deletion date:
```

Keep it with the participants' designated custodian, off the public website. Give
each affected participant access to their own relevant record and a correction
path. Share only what the decision requires. Agree a retention period and deletion
date before collecting details; retain longer only with a recorded reason and
participant notice. Publish aggregate shortages, task coverage and process failures,
without names or identifying narratives. No public incident or medical register.

## Try to break the procedure

Walk these cases through with participants before adoption. These are acceptance
cases for a proposed procedure, not field evidence that a pilot has succeeded.

| Case | Required outcome | The procedure fails if… |
|---|---|---|
| A person cannot lift because their back hurts. | Offer feasible work or rest; keep provision available. | Someone demands a diagnosis or calls inability refusal. |
| A caregiver did useful work that nobody saw. | Accept their account as self-reported; investigate only a material dispute. | An empty witness field becomes zero contribution or lost food. |
| Someone declines an understood, supported task. | Record that task's response and reassign the uncovered work. | The record becomes a debt, compelled labor or punishment by hunger. |
| The supply steward accuses a critic of theft. | Preserve evidence and hear both accounts through uninvolved reviewers. | The steward adjudicates alone, or criticism substitutes for evidence. |
| A person threatens another with a shared tool. | Protect the person at risk, restrict that tool if needed and set an expiring review. | Retaliation or an indefinite exclusion replaces protection and review. |
| Supplies cannot meet everyone's need. | Show the shortage and apply the pre-agreed need-based allocation rule. | Someone labels scarcity a contribution failure to hide it. |
| No independent reviewer is available. | Pause a new contested sanction; keep care and a current-risk response available. | The emergency becomes permanent or the review requirement disappears. |
| New evidence overturns a finding. | Correct the record, end the condition and plan repair. | The first decision becomes irreversible through pride or rank. |
